Fixed Goal Agent
Operates with a predefined goal throughout lifecycle.
Why it matters?
Predictable, simple, easy to validate.
How to decide?
  • Is the problem domain stable and well-defined?
  • Are objectives unlikely to change over time?
  • Is operational simplicity a priority?
What to do?
Clearly define the goal upfront.
What to avoid?
Rigid goals that ignore environmental changes.
Starter
Static goal set during setup/training.
Tools
Rule-based goal setting; scripted tasks.
Bottom line
Choose when requirements are stable and simple.
🔄
Adaptive Goal Agent
Adjusts or expands its goal based on context/feedback.
Why it matters?
Flexible and responsive to changing needs.
How to decide?
  • Does the agent need to adapt goals based on input or environment?
  • Are there identifiable triggers for goal updates?
  • Can some goal variability be tolerated?
What to do?
Define conditions/triggers for goal change.
What to avoid?
Goal drift; inconsistent updates.
Starter
Simple rule or policy for updating goals.
Tools
Feedback loops; reinforcement learning.
Bottom line
Choose when environment is dynamic.
🧠
Multi-Skill Agent
Equipped with multiple skills and selects them dynamically.
Why it matters?
Versatile and usable across many scenarios.
How to decide?
  • Will the agent handle multiple distinct tasks?
  • Is dynamic skill selection or composition required?
  • Is modular skill architecture feasible?
What to do?
Build modular, composable skill blocks.
What to avoid?
Skill conflicts or orchestration complexity.
Starter
Skill library with selection mechanism.
Tools
Skill management frameworks; skill APIs.
Bottom line
Choose when versatility is required.
🎯
Specialized Skill Agent
Focused on one deeply specialized skill.
Why it matters?
High accuracy and expertise in a narrow domain.
How to decide?
  • Is deep expertise in a narrow domain required?
  • Are performance and accuracy more important than generality?
  • Is ample domain-specific data available for tuning?
What to do?
Invest in rich domain-specific data/tuning.
What to avoid?
Overfitting, lack of generalization.
Starter
Single finely tuned skill module.
Tools
Domain-specific models; specialized datasets.
Bottom line
Choose when deep expertise is required.
